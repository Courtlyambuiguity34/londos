# Londos Business Model v0

## Objective

Keep Londos accessible to very small businesses while building a business capable of funding reliable infrastructure, trust, support and growth.

The working **UGX 5,000/month** figure should be treated as an entry-rent hypothesis, not a promise that every possible merchant workload can be served indefinitely for 5,000.

## What the merchant is buying

The merchant rents **digital commercial space in Londos**.

Included value can consist of:
- a persistent shop identity/address;
- presence in mall discovery/search;
- shop design capability;
- catalogue space within an allowance;
- optimized product media;
- merchant control panel;
- customer contact/commerce tools;
- basic analytics;
- platform security and maintenance;
- tenancy continuity while rent is current.

The language remains commercial-property language, not generic hosting plans.

## Revenue architecture

Londos should not depend on one revenue source forever.

### 1. Base rent

Recurring rent creates predictable revenue and aligns with the landlord model.

Working entry hypothesis:
- small shop: around UGX 5,000/month.

The actual included resource envelope must be measured in the pilot.

### 2. Larger commercial space / resource allowances

A merchant with 30 ordinary products and photos does not impose the same cost as a furniture showroom with thousands of products, videos and 3D assets.

Future rent can scale naturally with:
- catalogue capacity;
- media/storage allowance;
- showroom/media capabilities;
- staff seats/operational capabilities where justified.

Customer-facing pages should not advertise merchants as “Basic” or “Premium.” Those are tenancy/resource arrangements, not public status.

### 3. Optional promotion

Merchants may later pay for clearly labelled promotion:
- sponsored placement;
- category campaigns;
- mall events;
- launch/new-arrival promotion.

**Rule:** paid placement must be labelled and must not silently corrupt organic relevance.

### 4. Designer ecosystem

Professional shop designers can charge merchants one-time design fees. Londos may eventually charge a marketplace/referral/service fee if this ecosystem proves useful.

The 5K tenant should not require a paid designer to get a functional attractive shop.

### 5. Optional business services

Potential future services:
- advanced analytics;
- additional media/showroom processing;
- verified business services where verification itself has real cost/value;
- marketing tools;
- custom domain mapping;
- business messaging/CRM tools;
- logistics/payment integrations.

Do not add services merely to manufacture upsells. Each must solve a merchant problem.

### 6. Transaction revenue — optional, later

If Londos eventually offers integrated checkout/payment, transaction/service fees may become possible. This changes regulatory, fraud, dispute and operational exposure and therefore should be a deliberate later decision.

Base viability should not assume future payment custody will rescue an uneconomic rent model.

## Costs

### Variable
- payment collection;
- merchant verification;
- media storage/transformation/bandwidth;
- search;
- AI design;
- notifications;
- customer/merchant support;
- moderation/fraud;
- backups proportional to data.

### Semi-variable/fixed
- core hosting;
- observability/security tooling;
- staff;
- legal/compliance;
- sales/marketing;
- administration;
- engineering.

## Automation budget

At low rent, human attention is a scarce resource.

A useful metric is:

```text
Human support cost per merchant-month
= support minutes × fully-loaded support cost per minute
```

Pilot data should establish a support-minute budget compatible with contribution margin.

Automate first:
- onboarding guidance;
- shop setup;
- product imports where safe;
- media optimization;
- routine rent reminders;
- common support answers;
- basic moderation triage;
- shop-design iteration;
- payment reconciliation where integrated.

Keep humans for:
- identity ambiguity;
- fraud;
- appeals;
- unusual payment discrepancies;
- serious safety reports;
- account recovery;
- exceptional merchant support.

## Pricing guardrail

Do not offer “unlimited” resource consumption at the lowest rent.

A fair-use/resource policy protects all tenants and allows the platform to remain fast.

## Financial metrics

Track:
- MRR (monthly recurring rent/revenue);
- collected rent vs invoiced rent;
- payment failure rate;
- tenant churn;
- tenant renewal rate;
- ARPS (average revenue per shop);
- variable cost per active shop;
- contribution margin per shop;
- CAC (merchant acquisition cost);
- payback period;
- support minutes/shop/month;
- fraud/moderation cost;
- storage and bandwidth/shop;
- revenue concentration.

## Strategic test

Londos should be able to survive even if:
- most merchants remain on the lowest rent;
- integrated payments are never adopted;
- only a minority buy optional services.

If that is impossible, the entry rent or included resource envelope must change.
